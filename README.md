# Jodorowsky's Dune Bible

A reading edition of the *Dune* that Alejandro Jodorowsky prepared in the mid-1970s and never shot: the early draft of the screenplay, told scene by scene, with the surviving drawings and paintings beside it.

Open `index.html` in a browser. There is no build step and no server.

## What is in it

- **The Film.** All 153 numbered scenes of the early draft, in ten chapters. Each scene has its text on the left and its art on the right, every picture in the same plate format. A strip at the top of each chapter shows which scenes have art. Long scenes are folded to their first eight paragraphs until opened; one switch opens every scene in full. The contents page maps all 153 scenes at once and remembers where you stopped reading.
- **The Art.** All 448 plates from the image file, grouped: storyboard, pictures from the screenplay, costumes, machines, Giedi Prime, script pages, everything else.
- **Shots.** The whole film in order, one step at a time: 947 steps. 671 are shots, each beside its scene's script: storyboard frames and the screenplay's pictures. The other 276 are script cards. 87 scenes have no shot at all, most of the film after scene 48, so those scenes are told by cards of their own script, a few paragraphs to a card (about 70 words; a paragraph is never split, so a few cards are long and scroll). Nothing on a card is rewritten. The panel beside each step shows the scene's script with the card's lines marked, and notes where the typescript itself calls for a picture, linking to that picture if the archive has it. Where a sheet's printed grid could be trusted (49 sheets), `tools/frames.py` cut it into its frames, 467 in all, each with its caption strip at one size. 20 of those are frames the photograph cuts off, and 3 are frames whose ruled lines could not be found and are cut from the grid's estimate; each says so. On 22 more sheets (crops, strips, pasted-up collages) the grid cannot be fitted, so frames are searched for one at a time: 94 were found. Because that search can miss frames, those sheets appear whole first and their frames follow as close-ups. Nine sheets are shown whole only: five are single large drawings, and four gave fewer than two usable frames. A strip of ten segments, one per chapter and sized by its number of shots, shows where you are and jumps to any chapter. Links step to the previous and next scene. Arrow keys and, on a phone, swipes step shot by shot. Play advances by itself: seven seconds for a shot, and about a third of a second per word for a card, so there is time to read it. It waits while a plate is open or the tab is hidden and stops at the last step. The contents page offers the step you stopped on. Shots keep their addresses (`#shot-345`); cards are addressed by scene and card (`#script-100-3`), and `#script-100` goes to the scene's first step.
- **Sound.** Off until switched on. `assets/music.js` generates a score in the browser with the Web Audio API. Each chapter has its own root, scale, tempo and balance. What plays: a drone on the root and its fifth, whose bass note moves away and returns; the same harmony held as a chord two or three octaves up; a slow melody that builds its phrases from one short motif and comes to rest on the root or the fifth; bell notes; wind; and, when the intensity is raised, a drum. On the pages the intensity stays low and there is no drum. The films raise and lower it shot by shot. It is not a recording and not music made for the film; it was written for this edition and is covered by the repository's MIT licence like the rest of the code.
  The first version of the score had almost all of its sound below 300 Hz, which a phone's speaker cannot play: measured on the films, the part above 300 Hz sat about 40 dB down. The chord, the melody's register and the drum's upper note were added so that the music exists on a small speaker; the same measurement is now about 26 dB down, with the films levelled to -19 LUFS. Nobody has yet listened to it critically: it was built and balanced by measurement.
- **Watch.** `film/opening.mp4`: the opening shot, animated from its storyboard (1 min 52 s, 1920×1080, with sound). Sequence 1 of the storyboard is drawn as one continuous shot in 24 frames, and in most frames a small ruled rectangle marks where the camera travels next. `tools/film_opening.py` pushes into each frame toward its rectangle while the next drawing fades up inside it. The drawings and the captions are the storyboard's; the movement, timing, soft focus, grain, titles and sound are this edition's, and the film says so on screen. Eight frames have no rectangle; there the path is this edition's reading (`SHOT` in the script records each one). Nothing in it was drawn or generated by a model. The sound is `assets/music.js` recorded by `tools/record_score.js`.
  `film/harkonnen.mp4` (1 min 47 s) is the first film cut from a scene rather than from one continuous storyboard shot: scenes 39 and 40, the approach to the Harkonnen castle and the chess game on its balcony. `tools/film_scene.py` builds it from an edit list (`EDITS`): which picture, which part of it the camera starts and ends on, and which lines of the screenplay sit under it. The lines are the screenplay's, unaltered, split only where sentences end. Each sits under the storyboard frame whose own caption describes it; that pairing is this edition's. The pan up the castle and the push toward its head follow camera notes written on the drawing. Where a sheet survives in several scans, the sharpest is used. Adding a scene means adding an edit list. Before rendering, the tool checks every script line in the list against `assets/script.js` and stops if one is not word for word in its scene; `--stills` writes one still per shot to check an edit quickly.
  `film/museum.mp4` (3 min 11 s) and `film/history.mp4` (3 min 6 s) are the rest of chapter I after the opening shot: scenes 1 to 9 and 10 to 14, cut from the illustrated screenplay's own colour pictures, each shown at the scene the typescript calls for it. Their edit lists fetch the script's words from `assets/script.js` (`T(scene, block)`) instead of retyping them. Films longer than about three minutes are rendered to a near-lossless master and then encoded in two passes to a size the host will serve.
  `film/senate.mp4` (4 min 25 s), `film/emperor.mp4` (3 min 28 s) and `film/decision.mp4` (1 min 59 s) are chapter II, the Golden Planet: scenes 20 to 22, 27 to 29, and 30 and 31. Scenes 23 to 26 between them, the theft of the spice on Giedi One, have a single picture (the prison hole) and are not filmed; nor is the end of chapter I, scenes 15 to 19, which has only its title card. The storyboard here is a later version than the draft (a car for the wagon, a throne for the pregnant woman's stomach), and the end cards of the first and last say so. Three things were added to the tool for them: a line kind for titles the script itself superimposes (`'TITLE'`); a paper tone for a whole sheet used as a picture, so it matches the frames cut from sheets; and `Q(scene, block, words)`, which shows part of a block when one sentence is too long for the screen, and stops the render if those words are not in the block. The Emperor's statement in scene 31 is one sentence of about seventy words and is shown in five parts.
  Chapter III, Caladan, is five films: `film/caladan.mp4` (3 min 8 s, scenes 32 and 33), `film/jessica.mp4` (3 min 30 s, scenes 34 and 35), `film/blood.mp4` (2 min 35 s, scenes 36 and 37), and scene 38 in two parts, `film/trap.mp4` (2 min 52 s) and `film/gomjabbar.mp4` (4 min 46 s). The whole chapter is filmed. Scene 36 has no storyboard in the archive and is told in the script's words on black. Where the storyboard has no frame for a passage of scene 38, two of Moebius's costume designs stand in; the end cards say they are designs, not shots. Two frames of one sheet were never cut out singly and are taken from the sheet by position. The storyboard orders Jessica's memories differently from the draft (temple, then gift); the films follow the draft. With fourteen films, the Watch page lists them under their chapters.
  `film/baron.mp4` (4 min 5 s) is scene 41, the Baron's feast. It is the first film that leaves something out: one sentence, a dance the draft calls erotic with adolescents in it. The end card and the Watch page say so, and the script in the Film view is complete. Scene 42, in which Dr Yueh is broken by the torture of his fourteen-year-old wife, is not filmed for the same reason, and no storyboard for it is in the archive.
  `film/robot.mp4` (2 min 41 s) and `film/duel.mp4` (2 min 19 s) are scene 43, Paul's gymnasium, in two parts. The first two captions of the first part are the storyboard's own, shown in a dimmer colour, because the draft has no shot of the ship. Passages with no picture, including the end of the fight, appear as the script's words on black. Scene 43 goes on after the duel (Leto's lesson, the Voice); no storyboard for that survives in the archive and it is not filmed.
  From chapter VI on, the storyboard thins out: most scenes have one picture or none, and the Shots view tells them by script cards. The films go on only where there is enough to see. Scene 60, the flight over the desert, has the storyboard's twenty-fourth sequence and is three films: `film/shaihulud.mp4` (3 min 50 s), `film/harvester.mp4` (2 min 48 s) and `film/worm.mp4` (3 min 3 s). They use Chris Foss's ornithopter, H.R. Giger's worm and the screenplay's own drawing of the worm at the harvester. Two colourised copies of the storyboard are in the archive without a credit; they are not used.
  `film/wormanim.mp4` (1 min 51 s) is the first animated film: scene 60 again, in fourteen shots, made by `tools/animate.py`. It is cut-out animation. For each shot the dark lines of one storyboard frame are lifted off the paper, divided into parts by outlines set by hand (the ornithopter, the worm, the running men), and each part is moved, turned, scaled or bent over time, on twos, with a slight wobble of the line. No line is added and no generative model is used. The last shot does the same with the screenplay's colour drawing of the worm at the harvester; there the paper behind the harvester is filled in from its surroundings (OpenCV inpainting), which is the one place where pixels are made up, and the end card says so. The sound effects, wind, rotor, the worm, the explosion, footfalls, are synthesized in the same script from noise and sine waves, with no recordings, and mixed under the score. `--debug` writes a sheet of every shot's outlines and four poses. Each shot's outlines and movement are written by hand, so this suits key sequences with clean ink and separable parts, not every scene.
- **Find.** Search the screenplay; results quote the script's own lines and link to the scene.
- **The plate viewer.** Any picture opens large, with who made it, what was done to it, where it sits in the story and why, and a button to see the untouched scan.

Plate numbers match the page numbers of the source PDF.

## What was done to the pictures

The source is a 448-page PDF of scans collected online, mostly small. `tools/restore.py` makes a restored copy of each unique picture in `art/`. The originals in `plates/` are never changed.

- **Lighting evened** on storyboard sheets and cast sheets, so grey photographed paper becomes white.
- **Straightened**, for storyboard sheets photographed at an angle. The book's sheets carry a printed grid of frames, the same on every sheet, so `tools/grid.py` fits that grid to the frames it can find and undoes the camera's view. 21 sheets are corrected. A grid is used only when at least four frames in two rows and two columns agree and the frames it predicts are really there; flat scans and pasted-up collages are left alone.
- **Upscaled 4×** with Real-ESRGAN (`realesr-general-x4v3`), blended with a plain resize so the scan's own texture is not lost, then capped at 2,200 px on the long side.

Upscaling invents detail. The fine texture in a restored plate was produced by a model guessing from a small scan. Tiny typed captions do not become readable. Restored plates are labelled as restored everywhere they appear.

## What is original and what is this edition's

- **Words inside scenes:** the screenplay as transcribed, unaltered.
- **Chapter titles and one-line chapter descriptions:** this edition's.
- **Pictures placed by the screenplay:** 39 pictures matched to the typescript's own list of illustrations. Four of these matches were made by content and are labelled "likely". Three of the four were first matched to the wrong scenes and have been moved: plate 59 is the Harkonnen party of scene 41, not the revolt against the machines; plate 352 is Paul's bedroom in scene 56, not the barrier of the galaxy; and plate 157 is the Imperial Palace of scene 27, not the Harkonnen parade. The typescript's pictures for scenes 12, 14 and 84 are therefore missing from the archive. The fourth, plate 62 in scene 61, was checked again and stands.
- **Pictures placed by this edition:** everything else, including all the storyboard, placed from sequence numbers and captions (`PLACE` in `tools/catalog.py`). The storyboard is a later version of the film than this draft, so the fit is loose. Chapter II was first placed one sequence too early and has been corrected from the frames' own captions: sequence 4 is the Emperor's throne room (scene 29), sequence 5 his decision before the Senate (scene 31). The frames of Sardaukars and children, first put in scene 28, belong to the battle before the palace (scene 79). Chapter III has been re-read the same way: sequence 9 is the Emperor's gift of Jessica (scene 34), not the Duke's bedroom (scene 36), which has no storyboard in the archive; and one sheet of sequence 13, first put at the end of scene 38, is the Harkonnen castle (scene 39). In chapter IV, two loose pencil frames of the Baron made weightless move from scene 42 to the end of scene 41 (one certain, one likely); the other loose frames left in scene 42 could not be placed with confidence. Chapters V to X have not been re-read frame by frame.
- **Frames cut from sheets:** made by software from the restored sheets. The grid says roughly where a frame is; the cutter then finds that frame's own four ruled lines and cuts to them, because a photographed page curls. No drawn frame is dropped: if a frame's lines cannot be found it is cut from the estimate. The one exception is plate 92, whose bottom row is a typed colour legend, not frames. An automatic check of all 444 whole frames flags 40 where one side sits slightly off its line: those show the whole drawing but are tilted or show a sliver of the neighbouring frame. A shot is matched to its scene, not to particular lines of the script.
- **Gaps:** nothing was drawn or generated to fill scenes with no art. Those scenes are told by the script alone and say so, in the Film view and on the script cards in Shots. Pictures the typescript had but the archive lacks are listed by name.

## Other things that are uncertain

- 77 plates are repeat scans of a picture that appears elsewhere in the file. They are not restored and are listed at the end of The Art.
- Several costume identifications follow a fan-made chart in the file (plate 304).
- Five items appear to come from other Dune productions.
- The transcription has no scene 101, and one page headed "85 (continued)" with no scene 85 before it. Both are left as found.

## Rebuilding

```
python3 tools/build.py /path/to/Jodorowskys_Dune.pdf
python3 tools/restore.py /path/to/realesr-general-x4v3.pth
python3 tools/frames.py
python3 tools/build.py /path/to/Jodorowskys_Dune.pdf        # again, to pick up the restored plates
python3 tools/build_script.py "/path/to/Jodorowskys Dune Early Draft - Text.pdf"
```

The film is made separately, after `tools/frames.py`:

```
python3 tools/film_opening.py --cues cues.json                             # when the music should move
node tools/record_score.js 115 0 score.webm cues.json                      # the generated score, recorded in a headless browser while it follows the cues
python3 tools/film_opening.py /path/to/realesr-general-x4v3.pth /path/to/fonts out   # needs Anton.ttf, CourierPrime.ttf, CourierPrime-Bold.ttf
python3 tools/film_scene.py harkonnen /path/to/fonts out                               # then record the score in mood 3 and join it the same way
python3 tools/film_scene.py baron /path/to/fonts out                                   # mood 3 as well
python3 tools/film_scene.py robot /path/to/fonts out                                   # and `duel`; both use mood 4
python3 tools/film_scene.py shaihulud /path/to/fonts out                               # and `harvester`, `worm`; all use mood 5
python3 tools/animate.py /path/to/fonts out                                           # the animated film: picture, sound effects and score cues
python3 tools/film_scene.py museum /path/to/fonts out                                  # and `history`; both use mood 0
python3 tools/film_scene.py senate /path/to/fonts out                                  # and `emperor`, `decision`; all use mood 1
python3 tools/film_scene.py caladan /path/to/fonts out                                 # and `jessica`, `blood`, `trap`, `gomjabbar`; all use mood 2
ffmpeg -i out/opening-silent.mp4 -i score.webm -c:v copy -c:a aac -b:a 160k -af "volume=3.6dB,alimiter=limit=0.84:attack=5:release=80:level=false,afade=t=in:d=2.5,afade=t=out:st=105:d=6" -shortest film/opening.mp4
```

The scene films are scored the same way: `python3 tools/film_scene.py <name> <fonts> out --cues` writes `out/<name>-cues.json` from the film's `score` (an intensity for each shot, and accents: a hit, a swell, a drop), and prints the mood and length to record. The `volume` in the last command is whatever brings that recording to -19 LUFS.

Needs Python 3, Pillow and poppler (`pdfimages`, `pdftotext`). `restore.py` also needs PyTorch, NumPy and OpenCV, and takes about half an hour on four CPU cores.

- `tools/catalog.py` is the catalogue: titles, credits, sequences, captions, chapters, and where each picture is placed.
- `tools/sr.py` is a small runner for the upscaling model.
- `tools/grid.py` finds the printed grid on a storyboard sheet, and has a second, grid-free detector (`quads`) for sheets the grid does not fit; `tools/frames.py` uses it to cut sheets into frames (`frames/`, recorded in `tools/frames.json`).
- `tools/restored.json` records what was done to each plate.
- `view/` holds half-size copies of the restored plates (1,100 px) that the pages load in the flow; the viewer loads the full-size copy from `art/`. `tools/build.py` writes them.
- `tools/pack_hosted.py` bundles the original scans into a few files for hosts that cap the number of files per page. The repository itself serves `plates/` directly.

## Publishing

`wrangler.jsonc` publishes the repository root as a static site on Cloudflare Workers (`npx wrangler deploy`). One small Worker, `worker.js`, runs for `/film/*` only: static assets on Workers answer a byte-range request with the whole file, and Safari will not play a video without ranges, so the Worker returns just the bytes asked for. `.assetsignore` keeps `.git`, `tools/` and the docs out of the upload, `_headers` sets image caching, and `404.html` is the not-found page. Pushes to `main` deploy.
